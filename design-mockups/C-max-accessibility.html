Design rationale

  • Contrast first (AAA)Ink #1E1B2E on off-white and white on deep purple #3E1F99 both exceed 7:1. Bright purple and orange are used only for shapes and backgrounds, never for small text. Every card has a 3px dark outline, so edges stay visible with glasses or cataracts.
  • Text you can read without zoomingAtkinson Hyperlegible everywhere. Body text is 21–24px, and Agatha's answers and the live transcript are 27–32px bold. Medicine names always appear with the dose and an instruction in plain words ("with your meal").
  • One clear job per screenEach screen has one dominant button: TAP TO TALK, DONE, Yes I took it, Call 112. Everything else is secondary and outlined. Main buttons are full width and 68–150px tall, so a shaky finger can't miss.
  • Always say what is happeningA coloured state banner names the mode in words ("Agatha is listening. Tap DONE when you have finished", "Agatha is speaking out loud"). "You said" shows her words back, so a mishearing is caught before the answer is trusted. "Say it again" helps with hearing loss.
  • Every icon has a word, no hidden gesturesTabs and buttons all pair an icon with a label. Nothing needs a swipe, long press or double tap. SOS stays in the tab bar on every screen in red, and calls only start after a second confirmation. Agatha's face appears on every screen to keep it warm and personal, not clinical.
